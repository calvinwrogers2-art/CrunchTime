    How Crunch Time works

    Crunch Time is a daily play-calling game built on real NBA endings. Every day everyone gets the same spot from a real 2024-25 or 2025-26 regular-season game, picked up around two minutes left in the fourth: the real score, clock, timeouts and the ten players who were on the floor. You call every possession to the horn, then see what really happened.

    Calling the game

    On offense, pick one of eight sets built around the real players in your lineup, the tempo, and, inside the last two minutes, whether to burn a timeout to advance the ball. On defense, pick the coverage or decide to foul. Every possession is animated on the court with the real players and their numbers.

    Player ratings

    Each player gets a 2K-style overall and six attributes from his real season: Inside, Three, Free throw, Playmaking, Defense and Rebounding.

    Ratings drive the simulation. Shot percentages come from each shooter's real numbers. Your lineup's defense lowers what the other team shoots, your star's playmaking sets your turnover rate, and the rebounding gap decides who wins the offensive boards.

    Fast breaks, ankle breakers and dunks

    About a third of the time a shot misses and the defense grabs the rebound, they get out in transition. On a fast break you choose whether to push it to the rim, find the trailer for a three, or pull it out and set up. When they're running, you choose to sprint back, gamble for a steal, or take the foul. On dribble plays the ball handler breaks his defender's ankles about one time in ten, and the shot that follows always goes in. On plays that attack the rim, close to half of the shots are dunk attempts.

    Rebounds and second chances

    Who wins a missed shot depends on the two lineups' rebounding ratings, with extra weight on the bigs, and the player who grabs it is picked by his own rating. When your team gets an offensive board, you get a second-chance call: go back up, kick it out to your shooter for a three, or reset the offense. You have 3 seconds to decide. Wait too long and it's an offensive three-second violation and a turnover.

    How a call is graded

    Before you see what happens, the game plays out the rest of the game thousands of times for every option you had and compares your call with the best one. Win chance is what counts. A smart call that misses is still a smart call.

    Best call or within 1 point Within 3 points Costly, 3 to 6 points Big miss, more than 6

    Your Crunch Rating averages your calls on a 100 scale, and the percentile compares it with simulated play-callers facing the exact spots you faced. The clock follows NBA rules: inside two minutes it stops on made baskets and a timeout advances the ball. Ties at the horn go to overtime, decided as a coin flip.
